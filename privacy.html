Money Ledger keeps your entries on your own device. It has no server, no account, and no analytics. This page explains exactly what the one optional feature that touches the network — Google Drive sync — does with your data.

What data the app touches

By default, none of it leaves your browser. Your entries, categories, and settings live in your browser's localStorage and nowhere else.

If you choose to connect Google Drive sync, the app asks Google for the drive.file scope only. That scope lets an app see just the files it creates itself — it cannot see, list, or open anything else in your Drive.

What it's used for

Only one thing: syncing your own ledger between your own devices — for example, logging on your phone and reviewing on your laptop. The single file the app creates in your Drive holds your entries and is written and read only by Money Ledger running in your own browser.

Where it's stored

In two places you control: your browser's localStorage, and — if you connect sync — your own Google Drive.

There is no server. Nothing under the developer's control ever receives, stores, or is able to read your data. Sync happens directly between your browser and your Drive.

Sharing

Your data is never shared, sold, or transmitted to any third party. Nothing is collected beyond what the app needs to function, and nothing is used for advertising, profiling, or tracking.

Disconnecting

You can disconnect Drive sync at any time from the app's Sync & settings panel. Disconnecting never deletes your local data — your entries stay on the device. The file already in your Drive stays there until you delete it yourself.
